Advanced Decoy Steganography: The Ultimate Plausible Deniability

Welcome to the PixelCraft AI Advanced Decoy Steganography tool. Standard steganography hides a single message, but what happens if an adversary discovers the hidden data and forces you to hand over the decryption password? This is where our Decoy Steganography tool shines, providing mathematically guaranteed plausible deniability.

How Plausible Deniability Works

Our tool allows you to embed two entirely separate encrypted messages into a single image:

1. The Real Message: Your actual sensitive data, encrypted with a strong password.
2. The Decoy Message: A harmless or fake message, encrypted with a secondary "decoy" password.

If you are ever compromised and forced to decrypt the image, you simply provide the Decoy Password. The tool will decrypt the harmless message, and the adversary will have no cryptographic or statistical proof that a second, real message even exists within the same file.

Advanced QIM (Quantization Index Modulation)

To securely embed dual payloads without causing suspicious visual artifacts, we moved beyond outdated LSB techniques. The Advanced Decoy tool uses Quantization Index Modulation (QIM). This algorithm quantizes blocks of pixels (e.g., 8x8 pixel blocks) into discrete geometric zones. By carefully modulating these indices, we embed both the real and decoy payloads into the structural variance of the image. This method is incredibly robust against noise and minor compression, and it completely evades standard steganalysis tools that look for localized bit anomalies.

Military-Grade AES-256 Encryption

Both the real and decoy messages are independently encrypted using AES-256 cryptography before they are injected into the image via QIM. Because both ciphertexts appear as mathematically random noise, an attacker cannot differentiate between the encrypted data of the real message, the decoy message, or the natural background noise of a high-resolution photograph. 100% of this processing happens locally in your browser to guarantee zero server-side data leakage.
